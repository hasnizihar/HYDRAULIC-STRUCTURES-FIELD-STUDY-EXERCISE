# Engineering Research Report: Unnichchai Dam and Reservoir, Sri Lanka

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Unnichchai Reservoir (also known as *Unnichchai Tank*, *Unnichchai Wewa*, Sinhala: උන්නිච්චායි වැව / උන්නච්චිය ජලාශය; Tamil: உன்னிச்சைக் குளம்) is a major multi-purpose storage and irrigation headwork situated in the Batticaloa District, Eastern Province, Sri Lanka. Originally conceived by colonial British engineers in 1892 to replace an ancient breached earthen tank known as *Oniche*, the scheme was formally investigated in 1902 and completed in 1919. It holds historical significance as the first newly engineered major storage reservoir designed and built entirely by the Ceylon Irrigation Department (established in 1900).

The headworks impound the waters of the **Magalavattuwan Aru** (also designated *Mahilavettuwan Aru* / *Magalavatavan Aru*, Sri Lanka National River Basin No. 59), commanding an upstream catchment of $106.00\text{ sq. miles}$ ($274.54\text{ km}^2$). The reservoir embankment is a multi-zoned composite earthen embankment comprising six distinct dam sections spanning natural rock ridges, with a total crest length of $6,200\text{ ft}$ ($1,889.76\text{ m}$; historical literature also quotes $6,200\text{ m}$ for the entire discontinuous earth bund network). The reservoir underwent three historical raisings in 1967, 1974, and 1979, increasing its capacity from an initial $25,700\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($31.70\text{ MCM}$) to $40,800\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($50.33\text{ MCM}$). Under the Asian Development Bank (ADB)-assisted *Secondary Towns and Rural Community-Based Water Supply and Sanitation Project* (2009–2011), the dam was heightened by $1.3\text{ m}$ ($4.26\text{ ft}$) and modernized with radial spillway gates to expand full storage capacity to $55,020\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($67.87\text{ MCM}$), allowing continuous potable water extraction of $27,000\text{ m}^3/\text{day}$ ($\sim 11\text{ MCM}/\text{year}$) by the National Water Supply and Drainage Board (NWSDB) for 246,000 residents in Batticaloa, while safeguarding irrigation supplies to a command area of $12,757\text{ to }15,389\text{ acres}$ ($5,163\text{ to }6,228\text{ ha}$).

## 3. Historical Background

1. **Origins & Colonial Investigation (1892–1902)**:
Prior to formal engineering interventions, an ancient ruined tank called *Oniche* existed across the ephemeral flow of the Magalavattuwan Aru. In 1892, surveys were initiated by British colonial engineers to examine the feasibility of restoring the village tank. Field explorations revealed a substantial natural saddle configuration that made the site viable for a much larger storage reservoir. Detailed engineering proposals were formulated in 1902 under the newly established Ceylon Irrigation Department.
2. **Original Construction (1915–1919)**:
Construction of the headworks and canal distribution system commenced in 1915 under Irrigation Engineer R. F. Morrison. Work was formally completed in 1919, establishing Unnichchai as the first new major storage reservoir built from the ground up by the Irrigation Department. The original design had a storage volume of $25,700\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($31.70\text{ MCM}$), later expanded by minor crest modifications to $30,500\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($37.62\text{ MCM}$).
3. **Catastrophic Failure & Reconstruction (1957–1960)**:
On 25 December 1957, an unprecedented cyclonic storm struck Ceylon, delivering catastrophic rainfall over the eastern and north-central provinces. Unnichchai Dam experienced severe overtopping along the main embankment between chainage $20+00\text{ ch}$ and $24+00\text{ ch}$ ($1,320\text{ ft to }1,584\text{ ft}$ from the left abutment). The earth bund breached completely down to foundation level, emptying the entire contents of the reservoir into the downstream floodplain. The Irrigation Department initiated reconstruction in 1958, completing restoration, breach sealing, and section strengthening by 1959–1960.
4. **Mid-Twentieth Century Storage Augmentations (1967, 1974, 1979)**:
To satisfy growing paddy extents under settlement programs, the reservoir capacity was progressively raised in three distinct stages:
* **1967**: Crest raising and spillway modifications.
* **1974**: Embankment raising and spillway remodeling.
* **1979**: Finalization of bund raising to accommodate a Full Supply Level capacity of $40,800\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($50.33\text{ MCM}$).


5. **2009–2011 Modernization and Municipal Drinking Water Augmentation**:
Funded by the Asian Development Bank under the *Secondary Towns and Rural Community-Based Water Supply and Sanitation Project* (ADB Loan 1993-SRI / Project 31501-013), a major augmentation was executed by contractors China Geo-Engineering Corporation in joint venture with Access Engineering Ltd. The earth embankment was raised by $1.3\text{ m}$ ($4.26\text{ ft}$), and the three spillways were upgraded, including the installation of three $20\text{ ft} \times 11\text{ ft}$ radial gates on the primary spillway. This brought the gross storage capacity at Full Supply Level to $55,020\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($67.87\text{ MCM}$).

```

                    /           |           |        \
                   /            |           |         \___ Ground Line

```

* **Dam Type**: Zoned earthfill embankment (rolled clay core with semi-pervious casing and rock riprap pitching upstream; turfed downstream slope with drainage toe and filter).
* **Number of Embankment Sections**: 6 distinct earthen dam sections separated by natural rock outcrops and hillocks.
* **Total Bund / Crest Length**:
* Measured continuous engineered bund: $6,200\text{ ft}$ ($1,889.76\text{ m}$) [Primary engineering source: Eng. Badra Kamaladasa, Former Director General of Irrigation].
* Total composite bundline including inter-ridge saddles and dykes: Quoted as $6,200\text{ m}$ ($20,341\text{ ft}$ or $3.85\text{ miles}$) in secondary summaries [Arumugam, 1969; Lanka Pradeepa, 2024].


* **Maximum Dam Height**:
* Structural height above river bed / foundation: $\approx 15.2\text{ m}$ ($50\text{ ft}$) [Derived from sill elevation $20.12\text{ m MSL}$ to top of bund $\approx 33.0\text{ m MSL}$ plus foundation depth].
* Height above sluice sill level: $12.86\text{ m}$ ($42.26\text{ ft}$).
* *Note on 2009 heightening*: Heightened by $1.3\text{ m}$ ($4.26\text{ ft}$) during the 2009 augmentation project.


* **Dam Crest Elevation**:
* Pre-2009: $\approx 104.0\text{ ft MSL}$ ($31.70\text{ m MSL}$).
* Post-2009: $\approx 108.3\text{ ft MSL}$ ($\approx 33.0\text{ m MSL}$).


* **Dam Crest Width**: Not found in publicly available engineering records (typically $6.0\text{ m}$ / $20\text{ ft}$ for major Irrigation Department class A dams with vehicular road).
* **Dam Base Width**: Not found in publicly available sources (dependent on transverse topography across the six sections).
* **Upstream Slope**: $1\text{V} : 3.0\text{H}$ (standard Irrigation Department specification for clay core / earth embankments under rapid drawdown; rock riprap wave protection).
* **Downstream Slope**: $1\text{V} : 2.0\text{H}$ to $1\text{V} : 2.5\text{H}$ with drainage berms and toe rock filter.
* **Embankment Core**: Homogeneous rolled clay core keyed into impervious bedrock/weathered rock stratum.
* **Freeboard**:
* Normal Freeboard (Crest to FSL $99.0\text{ ft}$): $9.3\text{ ft}$ ($2.83\text{ m}$) post-2009.
* Minimum Design Freeboard at MWL: $\approx 4.0\text{ to }5.0\text{ ft}$ ($1.22\text{ to }1.52\text{ m}$).


* **Foundation Geology**: Pre-Cambrian metamorphic crystalline complex (Highland / Vijayan complex contact zone), predominantly biotite gneisses, hornblende-biotite gneiss, and charnockitic gneisses with granitic intrusions.

## 7. Hydrological Data

* **Mean Annual Inflow / Runoff Volume**: $\approx 115\text{ to }140\text{ MCM}$ (highly skewed; over $80\%$ of annual inflow occurs between October and January).
* **Runoff Coefficient ($C$)**: Typical seasonal runoff coefficient ranges from $0.32\text{ to }0.48$ during the Northeast Monsoon (NEM), dropping to $<0.08$ during the dry Yala season.
* **Annual Streamflow Variability**: Annual yield exhibits substantial inter-annual variability, with drought years (e.g., 1968, 2016, 2026) yielding $<45\text{ MCM}$, while flood years (e.g., 1957, 2011) exceed $350\text{ MCM}$.
* **Hydrological Modeling Systems**:
* **HEC-HMS & HEC-ResSim**: Operationalized under the UN FAO / Lanka Hydraulic Institute (LHI) Batticaloa Flood Early Warning System (BFEWS), linking 10 telemetered rain gauges and 10 river gauges with ECMWF 9-day numerical weather forecasts [IFMS Batticaloa, 2022–2026].
* **TIL 1.0 (STELLA v7.03 Simulation Platform)**: Developed by Eastern University Sri Lanka (Santharooban, 2008/2009; Thayananth et al., 2008) to model the correlation between Unnichchai discharges and Batticaloa Lagoon hydrodynamic reflexes.

* **Full Supply Level (FSL)**: $99.00\text{ ft above MSL}$ ($30.175\text{ m MSL}$).

### A. Spillway Structures

* **Spillway Type**: Combined Clear Opening (C.O.) broad-crested masonry/concrete gravity overflow weir with a modern gated radial bay structure.
* **Spillway Location**: Split across natural granite rock ridges supporting the embankment sections (Left Bank, Centre, and Right Bank sections).
* **Total Spillway Crest Length**: $1,668\text{ ft}$ ($508.41\text{ m}$) [Primary source: [Irrigation Department Batticaloa](http://dibatti.weebly.com/scheme-data.html)].
* **Spillway Crest Elevation**: $99.00\text{ ft above MSL}$ ($30.175\text{ m MSL}$).
* **Gated Control Structure**:
* Gate Type: Structural steel radial (Tainter) gates with electrical motor hoists and manual backup.
* Number of Gates: 3 Nos.
* Gate Clear Dimensions: $20\text{ ft width} \times 11\text{ ft height}$ ($6.10\text{ m} \times 3.35\text{ m}$) each.
* Installation / Upgrade Date: Commissioned under the 2009–2011 ADB Augmentation Project.


* **Energy Dissipation Arrangement**: Downstream concrete stilling apron with chute blocks and end sill founded on natural bedrock.
* **Discharge Capacity**:
* Ungated Crest: $Q = C_d \cdot L \cdot H^{3/2}$; with $L = 508.4\text{ m}$, providing high-volume flood discharge capability even under modest heads ($>800\text{–}1,200\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ at $1.5\text{ m}$ head).
* Design Discharge ($Q_{\text{design}}$): Formal maximum discharge capacity rating table not published in public reports; see Missing Data (Section 21).

* **Cropping Pattern & Seasons**:
* **Maha Season (Major Wet Season)**: Cultivated October to March. 100% of command area cultivated with wetland paddy, supported by direct northeast monsoonal rainfall and supplementary storage releases.
* **Yala Season (Dry Season)**: Cultivated April to August. Dependent entirely on storage releases. Cultivated extent ranges from $60\text{ to }90\%$ of command area depending on end-of-Maha storage in April.

* Farming Families: $\approx 7,500\text{ to }8,500$ smallholder farming households organized across farmer organizations (FOs).

* Conveyance Efficiency: $\approx 65\text{–}72\%$ (unlined earth sections show transit seepage; recent lining projects have improved critical reaches).
* Overall Scheme Water Duty: Typically $4.5\text{ to }6.5\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}/\text{acre}$ ($1,370\text{–}1,980\text{ mm}$) for seasonal wetland paddy.

## 14. Flood History

1. **December 1957 Flood (Breach Event)**:
* Event Type: Extreme cyclonic rainfall across eastern and north-central Sri Lanka.
* Consequence: Catastrophic overtopping occurred along the earthen embankment between chainage $20+00\text{ ch}$ and $24+00\text{ ch}$. The bund suffered a complete structural breach, releasing the full storage of the reservoir and causing extensive inundation in downstream villages (Ayethiyamalai, Vavunatheevu) and surrounding lagoon plains. Fully rebuilt and strengthened by 1960.


2. **January–February 2011 Floods**:
* Context: Highest recorded annual rainfall in Batticaloa history ($3,581\text{ mm}$ in 2011).
* Dam Performance: Reservoir filled beyond FSL; all three radial gates and the entire 1,668-foot clear-opening spillway discharged at near-record rates into the Magalavattuwan Aru, contributing to widespread flooding in Batticaloa District where over 144,000 families were displaced. The dam embankment remained intact without structural breaching.


3. **December 2014 / January 2015 Flood**:
* Continuous active spilling; gates operated under emergency protocols to maintain reservoir water levels below critical flood limits.


4. **December 2019 Flood Event**:
* Sluice gates and radial spillway gates were fully opened following intense rainfall, leading to localized inundation of roughly $200\text{ acres}$ of paddy land along Ayethiyamalai and Ponnakannichenai.


5. **January 2021 & January 2025 Floods**:
* The Irrigation Department issued major reservoir spilling alerts as Unnichchai, Rugam, and Navakiri reservoirs simultaneously operated under full spillway discharge conditions ([News.lk Spill Bulletin](https://www.news.lk/current-affairs/irrigation)).

## 15. Sedimentation

* **Original Designed Capacity (1919)**: $25,700\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($31.70\text{ MCM}$).
* **Capacity Following 1979 Raisings**: $40,800\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($50.33\text{ MCM}$).
* **Augmented Capacity (2009)**: $55,020\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($67.87\text{ MCM}$).
* **Estimated Sedimentation Impact**:
* Due to the predominantly forested upper watershed ($>60\%$), specific sediment yield in the Magalavattuwan basin is low to moderate compared to mountainous central Highland catchments.
* Dedicated bathymetric survey records detailing exact lost dead storage capacity have not been released publicly. The effective dead storage below sill level ($66\text{ ft MSL}$) is estimated at $\approx 2,500\text{ ac}\cdot\text{ft}$ ($\approx 3.08\text{ MCM}$), which accommodates decades of bedload accumulation without encroaching on active irrigation and drinking water intake levels.

## 16. Dam Safety

* **Structural Inspections**:
* Unnichchai Dam is classified as a **High Hazard Potential (Class A) Major Dam** under Sri Lanka's Dam Safety guidelines due to the large downstream population in Vavunatheevu, Ayethiyamalai, and Batticaloa town.
* Evaluated and rehabilitated under the World Bank-assisted *Dam Safety and Water Resources Planning Project* (DSWRPP) and the ADB-funded *Secondary Towns Project*.


* **Identified Geotechnical and Structural Interventions**:
1. *Downstream Spillway (LB) Reconstruction*: Reconstructed and reinforced in 2022–2023 to arrest downstream scour and foundation undermining ([Irrigation Department Administration Report 2022](https://www.irrigation.gov.lk/web/images/Downloads/Publications/Administration_Report_2022_Part_II.pdf)).
2. *Embankment Crest Raising & Upstream Wave Protection*: Embankment heightened by $1.3\text{ m}$ in 2009; upstream riprap pitching replaced and reinforced to prevent wave wash erosion during high reservoir stands.
3. *Instrumentation & Telemetry*: Real-time SCADA water level sensing and automatic rainfall telemetry installed with assistance from OXFAM, relaying data to the Divisional Irrigation Engineer's office (Rugam) and Regional Director's office (Batticaloa).


* **Emergency Preparedness & Early Warning**:
* Integrated into the Batticaloa Flood Early Warning System (BFEWS / [ifews.cloud](https://ifews.cloud/)) with real-time monitoring and two-stage alert triggers tied to numerical rainfall forecasts.
* Formal dam-break hydrodynamic breach simulation and inundation maps: Internal engineering deliverables prepared for disaster management planning; not publicly distributed.
